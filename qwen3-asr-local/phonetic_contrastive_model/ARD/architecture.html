Phonetic Contrastive Model · v1

A character-level Siamese encoder for Roman-Urdu normalisation

One shared encoder maps both a garbled variant and its canonical into the same 128-dimensional space; training pulls each variant next to its canonical, so the model learns the phonetic transformation (z↔j, aa↔a, ee↔i) instead of memorising spellings.

GRU(96 → 128) × 2, bidirectional
Reads the word forwards and backwards, in two stacked layers. Each character now carries the context of the whole word — that's how aa vs a and gh vs g become distinguishable.

Collapses the variable-length sequence into one fixed vector: mean captures overall shape, max the strongest features. Padding is masked out, so a 3-letter and a 12-letter word are pooled fairly.

Objective & runtime

Contrastive space — InfoNCE

Variants of a word cluster around their canonical; other canonicals are pushed away. In-batch negatives are taken over unique canonicals, so two variants sharing a canonical are never treated as negatives.

canonical its variants (pull in) other canonicals (push out)

Inference

resolve_word(w):

encode w → cosine vs the cached (1922, 128) canonical index → arg-max.

If best similarity < 0.60 → abstain (leave the word unchanged). This threshold is the safety guard that stops real words being corrupted.

A new canonical is added by encoding it once and appending to the index — no retraining.

char-level, not semantic — the signal is phonetic spelling, so a word/sentence embedder would be the wrong axis.  ·  Handles common-word drift & new canonicals.  Defers acoustically-confusable names (Siddique / Siddiqui) to audio.
